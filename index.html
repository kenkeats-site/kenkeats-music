Sonnet 1

Your dotted inanimate cathedrals, of oriental templar power
Still stand, as if some old dynasty in a long forgotten land

While others fret, in conscious coil,
I lost in wood and nook
Mind spun in drunken veil,
in and out of nascent night

Too herald coming age, as if written on holy page
In memory, youth and sun,
dancing in the street,
laughing at all I meet
Free, yet misunderstood, old rankled firewood

Laugh world, at failure to launch, never started
Take wing, set free, long departed
Judge not, the rhyme, so sweet in its prime
Like rosy roses, in cold crip dawn, untimely sawn

Old rooms, like old lovers, I know every crack
Your tear soaked carpet, your white walls washed
Your family, gone, dissolved by time
Your frame forced view, staring at sunset and star
I gone now, behind cosmic spin, off drifting far

To remain, never to leave, faithful to all
Everliving castle
When aged face falls, crumbled to ground
Your bricks piled high, your unassembled mess
Look kindly on your state,
Turn sorrow to gladness, at what life brings
Oh great monument, that graced herculean kings

Sonnet 27

Progenitor as question, thus I stand,
To breed and seed, that likeness may endure
When death doth come with cold and ruthless hand
To claim the mortal frame that must abjure.

I pluck no common lily from water bed,
But choose the flower of rare and greatest worth
One that sings and smiles with merry mirth,
Whose joy can lift the heaviest heart from dread.

She complements the suit that weak I wear,
And lends me strength where frailty holds its sway;
Oh closest companion, sought with fervent prayer,
The only one I seek by night and day.

In lovers’ lusty lane our bodies thrash,
In ecstasy or pain, with fire-rod flash.
He burns victorious, deep crimson red,
She lies vitreous upon the fevered bed;
Scarlet sinner with hidden blush displayed,
Their clothes in rushing haste to floor are laid.

He whispers soft, “Mine, yours, and ours shall be,”
Sweet bargains begging in her yielding ear;
Then somnolent, in dreamed desire we flee,
Consecration born of fury’s fire, her and me

Now closer stand I to death’s darkened door,
No likeness yet doth roam this earthly floor.
Behold the chance! The mirror tells no lie
My image preserved, never to die.

Sonnet 28

When midnight strikes upon the final hour,
tired eyes surrender all their judging power.
Day’s chorus fades, voices, scenes, life’s tiring play,
Characters corralling, as they may.

Sojourn in Paris with the self-involved French
Reveals my own soul as a restless wench.
A snooty waiter, oily with practiced charm,
Keeps me balanced on edge, eating from his palm.

Meeting the women from each flavoured state
Nordic eyes offer forgiveness for feminine frustration,
Blue eyes fade with constant migration.

By grand canals I sip the ruby wine,
While murky waters whisper poverty’s decline.
Still do I feel exclusive, playing fool,
Ever lost among the drunken mules.

Too often I’ve wandered in and out of love,
I know the game as fits a well-worn glove.
Casanova’s ease is easy to admire;
Yet guilt’s hot flames set all my heart afire.

Laurels of loneliness gather at my door.
I sweep them softly across the mezzanine floor.
Who remains back home? Who hasn’t died?
Most are married now, children by their side.

My ancient preacher has forgotten my name.
Scarce I myself know the creature I became:
Cunning, ruthless, armed with charm’s sharp blade,
Capable of anything, yet loathe to fade.

But when I weigh the sum of what I am,
I see the strangers met across the land,
The helpful hand, the lovers in the park,
The beggar praying, the artist’s fervent spark.
Then comes the gentle, devastating key:
In the end all these people were only me.

Sonnet 29

Beneath a Croatian sun, that titan eye
Has left Taurus’ horned moon alone,
There lies a field where Flora’s bosom swells
With sensual riot, flowers drunk do dwell
A night-nymph trips the dewy beds in heat,
Comely and ravenous, her footsteps sweep
To some wild music older than the stars.
Men wander, fevered in fond hope
For glance, a tryst, or else the hangman’s rope.

Once, proud princess now chaste with desire
Married to self, the musical messiah
Emblazoned union of creative force,
Where stars themselves burn true and chart true north
Where others breathe but frost and ashen grey,
Snowdrops thrust white heads on Helsinki’s frozen day;
The city sleeps, its golden maids lie still,
While one lone wolf goes roaming, hunger-fed.

How many ports have I, false lover, left?
How many ships have drawn their sighing wakes
Across the brine, my only faithful mate?
Yet since that time, mine own, I have beheld
A world in vernal glory: rapeseed’s veil
Of living gold that mocks the very sun,
Chestnuts in milky candelabra hung,
And beech on beech, feathery legions run.

A Roman road, all cobbled, cracked, and torn,
Dappled with leaf-light, young birds being born.
I heard you calling ’neath nature thus seen,
I dare not speak your name, so I will call you my dear miss Green.

Sonnet 30

When autumn fall besets the city
And winter’s wind doth strip the evergreens,
Remember, Oh remember, youth’s raw fear:
Those days that asked no question of the light,
But held the commonwealth of flowers in sight.

Its gone, its gone! Can vagrant odours wake
The sleeping past? The jasmine’s evening star,
The ocean’s salted breath, the eucalyptus oil
Yet all, alas, like ashes on the wind disperse.

Go home, go home! Before you forget
Summers sting, burnt desire, will it come again
The crimson banksia tells its steadfast tale.
Will you fuss or fret, or vent base wail;
Be silent, soul, and learn acceptance grace

For none may forestall the appointed hour.
Momento mori morning solemn pledge
Forgotten at nights moon light edge
Oh dusty Death, enfranchise them; set free!
The ocean opens, Moses beside me.

Youth, the fleet fox, hath slipped the mortal snare;
I face the father form within the Ithacan storm
Unhoused, adrift, by adult terrors pressed
Yet know not how; but Time, in mercy, speaks.
God’s way? As mother’s voice, long echoing, says:
‘Dear child, I hope you pray’

The Rambler

I was lost down a ditch early in life, half-buried in the dirt of bad choices and worse luck, when a drifter wandered by and handed me a tambourine. That simple gift rattled something awake inside me. I hitched rides out east, chasing the dream of the circus ring, but they turned me away at the gate, no room for a ragged kid with no name and no tricks. So I begged on main street for scraps, until a woman from hard times took me in. She showed me what women could do when the world tried to break them, and in her place I picked up a guitar for the first time. From there I began to ramble, searching out souls who still knew the real beat of life: Blind Willie’s haunting slide, Robert Johnson’s crossroads howl, the forgotten ones who sang because they had to.

I played late-night bars down by the wharf, where sailors fresh off the ships would stagger in, salt-crusted and restless. If my set went sour, one of them would pull me aside in the dim light and growl, “You gotta know how to play your axe, boy.” That was the lesson that stuck. I scrimped and saved my way back home, carrying scars and songs that finally found their shape.

The music that came out of those years draws deep from the great songwriters of the last century, Dylan, Young, Springsteen, and their kin, centering on thought-provoking lyrics and melodies that twist in unexpected ways. Solo, it’s sweet folk: gentle arrangements wrapped around stories at the core of the human experience, love, loss, the long road, the quiet ache. With the band, it shifts to a folk-rock edge, each song a different shade, a different story, full of grit and lift.

For the last four years I’ve played solo around Perth, honing the craft in small rooms and hidden stages, learning what lands and what lingers. In 2013 the band came together, called the Lucid Dream, and the sound found its full shape.

That’s the path so far: from a ditch to a stage, from a tambourine to this. The road keeps going.
